Alerts are for the moment something happens. Most of what cameras see doesn't need a ping at all. It needs to be in the recap. The delivery that turned up early, the door that was propped open for an hour, the quiet afternoon that wasn't. Today nobody writes that recap, because writing it would mean watching the whole day back, and nobody has that kind of afternoon to spare.

## What lands in the summary

Everything that mattered, in plain English. The near-miss at the loading dock. The queue that hit 6 people at lunchtime. The delivery that arrived while the manager was off site. Because a vision model has described every frame as it happened (people, objects, actions, hazards), a written recap of the day is possible at all, and anything in it that deserves a closer look links into the footage, which stays [searchable](https://svid.ai/guides/how-to-search-through-hours-of-cctv-footage) after the fact.

## 2 very different readers, same mechanism

For a site or duty manager, it's the shift recap: what happened across the site today, readable in 2 minutes at close. For a nursery, the same mechanism answers a warmer question, the one every parent asks at pickup: what did my child do today? Same cameras, same pipeline, a very different email. If your version of the question is somewhere in between, it probably fits too.

## Summaries plus alerts, not instead of

The summary doesn't replace being told about serious things at once. Alerting checks judge a window of recent frames as footage comes in, so a fall or a concealment reads as an event rather than a single suspicious frame, and the alert goes out on screen, by email, or by JSON webhook while it still matters. One event fires one alert. The same check firing again within 2 minutes is recorded as a continuation, not a new siren. The daily summary is the layer underneath: everything that can wait until the end of the day, in one email instead of 40 pings.
