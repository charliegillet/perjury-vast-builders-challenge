# Micron or Oracle: One AI Stock Is a Winner, the Other a Mistake, Says Top Investor

[![Ben Yoffe](https://blog.tipranks.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/cropped-BYT-96x96.png)\\
Ben YoffeOct 01, 2026, 07:42 PM](https://www.tipranks.com/news/author/ben-yoffe)

A-

![Micron or Oracle: One AI Stock Is a Winner, the Other a Mistake, Says Top Investor](https://blog.tipranks.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/MU-ORCL-750x406.jpg)

**Micron ( [NASDAQ:MU](https://www.tipranks.com/stocks/mu/forecast))** and **Oracle ( [NYSE:ORCL](https://www.tipranks.com/stocks/orcl/forecast))** offer investors two very different routes into AI. Micron supplies memory needed inside AI accelerators and data centers, while Oracle is spending heavily to expand cloud infrastructure supporting training and inference.

Recent developments have pushed their stories further apart, despite booming demand surrounding both companies. Micron closed fiscal 2026 with record quarterly revenue of $54.23 billion, up 379% year-over-year, while adjusted EPS reached $33.42. Oracle, meanwhile, reported 121% cloud infrastructure revenue growth during its latest quarter, yet concerns surrounding heavy capital spending and debt continue weighing on investor sentiment.

One top investor, known by the pseudonym The Techie and ranked among the top 3% of stock experts on TipRanks, sees very different prospects for these two AI names.

Micron entered its latest report facing extremely high expectations, leaving little room for disappointment. Yet, Techie came away believing investors still have reasons to remain optimistic about future returns.

“I’m maintaining the stock with a Buy at current levels and see more upside ahead now that the pressure of earnings is behind us,” the investor wrote.

Memory demand represents an important part of that optimism, especially as AI moves deeper into inference. “The next leg of AI demand is more memory-hungry than the last one, in my opinion,” Techie explained. He added that “Training was a compute story, but inference at scale, especially agentic inference, is increasingly a memory story.”

That transition could give Micron several avenues for growth beyond simply selling additional HBM capacity. Longer agent tasks require greater memory content within accelerators, alongside additional DRAM and storage throughout data centers. Meanwhile, constrained industry supply could support pricing if customers shift capacity between premium HBM products and conventional alternatives.

Valuation gives Techie another reason to stay bullish after Micron’s latest results. Forward non-GAAP P/E stands at 6.60 times, compared with a sector median of 23.30 times, while forward EV/EBITDA sits at 4.92 times versus 14.31 times.

Oracle receives a far less enthusiastic verdict after Techie’s earlier bullish calls failed. He initiated coverage around $306 with a Buy recommendation, then reiterated his optimism several months later before shares continued falling. “One look at the chart, and you can tell the call was wrong,” Techie admitted.

That disappointing performance has now prompted him to abandon his previous bullish rating. “I’m downgrading the stock to a Hold at current levels, as AI-related uncertainty remains an overhang on the stock and limits any significant upside ahead,” the investor explained.

Oracle’s problem, from Techie’s perspective, goes beyond whether quarterly results can surpass Wall Street forecasts. Investors have remained concerned about financial commitments accompanying its aggressive cloud expansion, changing how strong earnings reports are received. “At the time, I was handicapping the results, while the market was handicapping the balance sheet,” he acknowledged.

Still, Oracle has several opportunities to regain Techie’s confidence during coming quarters. The investor wants evidence that older GPUs retain attractive renewal terms through Nvidia’s Vera Rubin transition, while upcoming results could provide another test. “A clean beat against an already-lowered bar is plausible in my opinion,” he said.

For now, however, The Techie’s preference leaves little ambiguity between these two stocks. Micron retains his Buy rating as memory consumption expands alongside AI, whereas Oracle must prove its costly cloud expansion can translate into returns that justify taking another bullish position.

Wall Street, however, doesn’t think investors necessarily have to choose between the two. Both stocks carry a Strong Buy consensus rating, with Micron earning 24 Buys and one Hold, while Oracle receives 27 Buys, 4 Holds, and one Sell. Their average price targets also leave considerable room for gains, with Micron’s $1,563.96 target implying 42.50% upside and Oracle’s $245.75 forecast pointing to 78% upside over the next 12 months.
